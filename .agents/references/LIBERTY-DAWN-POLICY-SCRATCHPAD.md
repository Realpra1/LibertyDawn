# Persistent policy notes

- For fragile, high-value Covert raiders such as Stealth Tanks, a safe compatible repair cycle is generally sensible when it returns the unit to a useful harassment group; survival and post-repair action matter more than merely reaching full health.
- Do not treat one protected repair fixture as proof that recovery beats immediate action in ordinary play. Compare time to useful re-engagement, survival, and exchange value in natural or adversarial full-AI games.
- A danger screen should not be relaxed solely because it rejects targets often. For fragile stealth raiders, test apparent over-conservatism against known escape paths, detection, and defensive coverage; safe avoidance may be correct.
- Fixture-disabled economies can expose suspicious states but do not establish an AI-policy defect without reproduction in an economy-enabled game.
- Failsafe fallback to an ordinary manager is strategically sensible only as a bounded survival mechanism: evaluate registry ownership and safe control separately from the advanced policy that shedding prevents from operating.
- For covert harassment, compare a detector-covered target with a reachable low-coverage target and a credible escape route; survival and useful damage distinguish sound preservation from blanket over-conservatism.
